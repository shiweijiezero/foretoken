These tasks define goals, scope, constraints, and deliverables. Use the [task template](../templates/task-template.md) to specify the model, resources, workloads, and objectives. Follow the [shared workflow](../README.md#steps), using [meta-knowledge](../guidance/meta-knowledge/README.md) for research methods and the [knowledge library](../guidance/knowledge/README.md) for technical principles and experience.

## Autonomous execution

Continue working within the task scope and resource budget. Judge completion by the objective and measured results. The lead develops directions and owns environment preparation, deployment and parallel experiments, and final acceptance. Workers research and develop candidates and analyze returned results. Follow the [shared role contracts](../../.agents/skills/rsi/SKILL.md#set-up-the-team-and-assignments) for execution and collaboration.

- When earlier experiments exist, read their records, check the conditions, and continue from retained approaches, ruled-out directions and unresolved questions. Reuse comparable results and spend resources on new questions; if changed conditions or insufficient evidence call for a repeat, state what it should establish.
- Reuse existing tools for building, deploying, measuring, recording, and comparing. For recurring obstacles, the lead distinguishes usage and environment issues from framework defects, assigns worthwhile code fixes to Workers within the task scope, and validates the affected workflow before reuse.
- Carry investigation, design, implementation, deployment, evaluation, and analysis through to completion. During investigation, consult relevant papers, official documentation, and upstream practices online, and develop an approach grounded in the current implementation. Resolve problems that can be handled independently and proceed with established next steps.
- Use evidence to choose the next action after each iteration. Investigate ineffective approaches, refine or replace them, and assess the remaining gap to the objective after a partial improvement.
- Include every node listed in the task in resource planning. Check available GPUs, CPU cores, and memory on each node, then allocate independent candidates according to their requirements. Track experiment progress and GPU utilization across all nodes, and schedule experiments when resources and candidates are available. Record why any node is unused and resolve actionable blockers. Coordinate shared resources to keep results comparable.
- Maintain useful work within the task's parallelism requirements throughout execution. Process subagent results and assign follow-up work as agents finish; schedule the next candidate as experiments finish. Advance independent investigation and implementation while measurements run, and reassign work when blocked. Count subagents as active while they are executing tasks.
- Delegate self-contained research questions with scope, budget, and expected development outcomes. Workers edit independent candidate worktrees; the lead synchronizes and runs each delivered version from its corresponding checkout. Keep running source unchanged, continuing development elsewhere or after the lead releases the checkout. Members keep collaborating after handoffs and share findings that affect others. When a question is answered or cannot advance within scope, the lead arranges follow-up and cross-direction integration.
- Each experiment answers a concrete question and informs a decision. Reuse results that already answer it; choose targeted comparisons for unresolved explanations. Use enough requests or measurement time to make that judgment, increasing samples or repeats when variation, tail latency, or stability needs closer evaluation.
- Treat code changes, service startup, and individual test runs as progress rather than task completion. Support conclusions with measurements under actual workloads; evaluate answer quality when changes affect generation behavior.
- Update records and reports, validate retained changes, and clean up unused resources throughout iteration, alongside independent research and experiments. Near the deadline, choose useful work that can finish within the remaining budget based on observed durations and dependencies, including analysis and delivery. Keep work moving until the task's end conditions are met. At completion, state which objectives were met, which changes were retained, and where results are stored; identify remaining blockers and what is needed to resume.
